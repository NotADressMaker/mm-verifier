# Informational relativity

Informational relativity describes the fact that evidence-bounded assessments
may differ under declared policies, interpretations, authorities, jurisdictions,
and times. It does not borrow validity from physical relativity. Legitimate
variation depends on public rule semantics, congruence between declared and
executed rules, stable recognition conditions, attributable verification
authority, and prospective clarity about how rule changes affect an assessment.

In MAMV, policy and interpretation are mutable primary rules. Claim and evidence
bindings, policy resolution, verifier attribution and authority, recognized
schema, and receipt integrity are recognition conditions: a failure makes an
artifact structurally invalid rather than evidentially unsupported. See
[Assessment rules](ASSESSMENT_RULES.md), [Assessment validity](ASSESSMENT_VALIDITY.md),
and [Frame prediction](FRAME_PREDICTION.md).
